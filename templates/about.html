About MedScan Assist

Architecture, Dataset Provenance, Algorithmic Interpretability & Clinical Safety Disclaimers

1. Research Purpose

MedScan Assist was engineered as an open, reproducible medical machine learning benchmark prototype. The project investigates how modern transfer learning models perform when tasked with chest radiography triage and how visual interpretability tools like Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) can help engineers inspect decision boundaries.

Modern computer vision models often achieve high statistical accuracy on public benchmarks while inadvertently relying on spurious background shortcuts (such as hospital tokens, patient orientation markers, or chest tube artifacts). By synthesizing probabilistic predictions, image quality metrics, and spatial saliency heatmaps, this interface enables human-in-the-loop inspection.

2. Deep Learning Architecture & Training

The underlying classification backbone is built on EfficientNet-B0, chosen for its compound scaling coefficient that balances parameter efficiency and feature extraction fidelity:

  • Feature Extraction: Depthwise separable convolutions pre-trained on ImageNet-1k, fine-tuned on pulmonary radiographic patterns.
  • Regularization: Dropout rate of 0.2 prior to the linear classification layer to mitigate memorization.
  • Input Pipeline: Standardized 224×224 resolution with three-channel normalization matching ImageNet distributions.
  • Interpretability Layer: Grad-CAM gradients tapped from the final convolutional block (e.g. features[-1]) prior to global average pooling.

3. Dataset Provenance

The baseline model is trained and validated on the Kaggle Chest X-Ray Images (Pneumonia) repository, consisting of pediatric chest radiographs collected at the Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center.

Cohort Profile: Pediatric patients aged 1 to 5 years old. Ground-truth labels were established by initial radiologist evaluations and further quality-checked by senior clinical graders.

4. Important Clinical & Technical Limitations

Do not use in medical facilities: This system is not cleared by FDA, CE, or health authorities for diagnostic, screening, or treatment selection purposes.
  • Demographic Generalization: Performance on adult or geriatric cohorts is unverified and likely subject to severe demographic distribution shift.
  • Projection Specificity: The pipeline is restricted to frontal chest radiographs (Anteroposterior / Posteroanterior). Lateral or oblique projections will yield invalid predictions.
  • Comorbidities & Confounding Pathologies: The model operates on a binary classification regime (Normal vs. Pneumonia) and does not reliably distinguish other thoracic diseases (e.g., COVID-19, tuberculosis, pneumothorax, pulmonary nodules, cardiomegaly).
  • Resolution Downsampling: High-resolution DICOM radiography (often 2048×2048 or higher) is compressed to 224×224 for neural network processing, sacrificing fine micro-trabecular details.
  • Interpretability Caveat: Grad-CAM highlights coarse regional feature focus, not pathological tissue borders.

5. Data Privacy & Ethics

MedScan Assist enforces non-retention policies during demo executions. Images uploaded are held in temporary memory solely for the duration of model inference and saliency map creation. No personal identifiable information (PII) or protected health information (PHI) is logged, tracked, or indexed.
